This morning I walked without headphones. The city was doing its ordinary work:
shops opening, bicycles leaning against narrow trees, a delivery cart rattling
over stone.

Unstructured time is difficult to defend because it rarely produces anything
immediate. But I keep noticing that the best thoughts arrive after the mind has
stopped bargaining with the clock. A walk is not a method, exactly. It is a
permission structure.

I came home with no revelation, just a softer sense of proportion. That was
enough.
